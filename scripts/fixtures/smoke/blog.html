Seven Days in the Spiti Valley

By Priya Raman · 14 July 2026 · 9 min read

The Spiti Valley is a cold desert mountain valley located high in the Himalayas. This is the account of a week spent between monasteries, homestays and impossibly blue skies.

Our route began in Shimla, wound through Kinnaur, and crossed the Kunzum Pass at 4,551 metres before descending into Kaza, the valley's main town.

The itinerary

We kept days short — altitude is a demanding companion — but the density of experience was unmatched:

Every homestay offered the same warm formula: butter tea, stories of the winter, and apricot jam straight from the orchard.

"Spiti doesn't reveal itself to the hurried traveller. Sit still long enough and the mountains begin to talk."

Pack responsibly

The valley is fragile. Carry your waste out, refill bottles, and choose homestays over camps where possible.

Common questions

When is the best season to visit?

June to September. The Kunzum route is snow-bound for the rest of the year, and even summer nights dip close to freezing.

Do I need permits?

Indian nationals need none for Spiti itself. Foreign nationals require an Inner Line Permit for stretches near the border, easily arranged in Kaza.

Is the trip suitable for children?

Villages like Tabo and Kaza are comfortable with kids if you plan altitude gains slowly and build in rest days.
